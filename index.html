HiQ-Crawl

RL-based Hierarchical Humanoid Quadrupedal Crawling for Scalable Confined Space Traversal

Abstract

Humanoid locomotion in confined spaces requires continuous height adaptation across multiple locomotion modes, yet most existing approaches remain limited to height-adjustable bipedal walking. We present HiQ-Crawl, an RL-based hierarchical framework that enables a Unitree G1 humanoid to traverse height-constrained environments through variable-height bipedal walking and quadrupedal crawling. The learned policies continuously regulate body height over complementary ranges, from 1.27–0.50 m in bipedal locomotion to 0.50–0.30 m in crawling, substantially extending the minimum navigable clearance. To address the contact-rich dynamics and unmodeled disturbances inherent in quadrupedal crawling, we adapt a history-conditioned latent representation that implicitly captures terrain and interaction context from proprioceptive observations, improving terrain adaptation and sim-to-real robustness. A bidirectional transition policy connects crouched bipedal locomotion and quadrupedal crawling, enabling smooth and stable switching despite large kinematic and contact discontinuities. We further integrate these skills with an adapted model-based planner that converts LiDAR point clouds into collision-free three-dimensional paths and locomotion commands, including proactive height targets. Simulation studies, ablations, and real-world experiments demonstrate accurate height and velocity tracking, robust traversal over complex terrain, reliable mode transitions, and autonomous navigation through highly confined spaces. HiQ-Crawl provides a scalable locomotion framework for deploying humanoid robots in complex, low-clearance environments.

Method

Hierarchical control for confined-space traversal: bidirectional mode transitions, history-conditioned latent policies, and LiDAR-driven planning.

Demos

Real-world deployments on the Unitree G1 across whole-trajectory navigation, variable-height walking, crawling, and scalable confined traversal.
